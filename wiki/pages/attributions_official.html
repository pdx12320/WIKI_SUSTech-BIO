Dengxiang Yang
Student Leader
Tasks
Analysis, Conceptualization, Background Research, Investigation, Data Curation, Notebook/record keeping, Project Administration, Public Engagement, Software, Wiki Coding, Visualization, Writing, Safety
Specific Tasks
(1) Analysis – He analyzed PUF–APOBEC editing at C388, C295, and C871, examining editing measurements alongside computational features to assess predictive performance. He compared candidate feature sets and evaluated their usefulness for modeling editing outcomes. He contributed to fine-tuning and testing the Lamar model and interpreted its predictions in relation to experimental evidence. He also analyzed RNA-seq editing results, considering both on-target activity and potential off-target effects to support candidate comparison and identify questions for further experimental investigation. (2) Conceptualization – He helped shape the dry-lab research strategy by defining prediction goals and candidate selection criteria for the PUF–APOBEC project. He considered how sequence information, predicted structures, and experimental editing measurements could contribute to candidate evaluation. He incorporated both on-target and off-target editing into the assessment of PUF performance and helped translate computational predictions into candidate priorities and experimentally testable questions. His work connected computational development with the project’s wet-lab objectives. (3) Background Research – He reviewed the literature on PUF repeat architecture, APOBEC deaminases, and protein–RNA interactions to establish the scientific basis for the dry-lab work. He examined how existing knowledge could inform feature selection, candidate comparison, and structural interpretation. He also studied AI-based protein design approaches to assess their relevance to the project and support tool selection. His research informed the computational workflow and helped explain the rationale and limitations of the methods presented in the Wiki. (4) Investigation – He clustered APOBEC variants to organize and compare candidates and performed protein design using RFdiffusion, ProteinMPNN, and LigandMPNN. He used AlphaFold to evaluate structural confidence and examine structural properties of the designed proteins. He interpreted these computational outputs alongside the project’s candidate selection criteria to support further investigation. Throughout this work, he treated predicted structures as supporting evidence rather than experimental confirmation of protein function. (5) Data Curation – He organized mutation sequences, editing measurements, predicted structures, and residue-contact features into datasets for computational analysis. He standardized sample identifiers and merged experimental records to maintain consistent links between candidate sequences and their associated measurements. He prepared these datasets for model training, evaluation, and feature-set comparisons. His curation work maintained consistency between experimental records, modeling inputs, and reported results, providing a traceable foundation for subsequent analysis. (6) Notebook / Record Keeping – He maintained records of dataset versions, computational parameters, modeling outputs, and workflow updates throughout the dry-lab project. He documented changes to analysis and software workflows so that results could be traced to their corresponding inputs and settings. These records supported comparisons between modeling iterations and helped team members understand the development of the computational work. His documentation also supported method descriptions, internal handovers, and reproducible dry-lab workflows. (7) Project Administration – As the dry-lab student leader, he coordinated computational research and software development across the team. He assigned tasks, tracked progress, and aligned individual contributions with the project’s scientific objectives. He communicated with the wet-lab team to exchange results, clarify computational needs, and coordinate candidate evaluation with experimental schedules. He also brought together modeling, RNA-seq analysis, software development, and Wiki preparation, supporting continuity between research activities and the team’s final deliverables. (8) Public Engagement – He participated in synthetic biology outreach at the Affiliated High School of SUSTech, helping communicate the project’s scientific background to a broader audience. He contributed educational webpage content explaining concepts related to synthetic biology and protein engineering. He also joined Synbio Challenges to share knowledge and engage with others interested in the field. Across these activities, he translated technical material into accessible explanations for educational and public-facing settings. (9) Software – He developed and refined the computational framework supporting the project’s dry-lab analyses. He built and optimized the RNA-seq pipeline for editing detection, filtering, quantification, and annotation. He integrated multiple models and tools into a unified workflow, connecting data preparation with computational analysis and result interpretation. He also contributed to software testing and iterative refinement to support consistent workflow use. This work provided computational infrastructure for evaluating editing results and preparing outputs for further analysis and presentation. (10) Wiki Coding – He coded and maintained the Dry Lab Wiki page through GitLab, organizing its layout and navigation to present the computational work clearly. He embedded figures, analysis results, and supporting explanations, connecting the project’s methods with their corresponding outputs. He resolved display issues and maintained version control as the dry-lab content evolved. His work turned computational records and findings into a navigable resource for judges and other readers. (11) Visualization – He created model-performance plots, candidate-ranking charts, and protein structural images for the Wiki and project presentations. These visualizations communicated comparisons among computational models, candidate proteins, and predicted structural features. He organized figures to support the scientific narrative and clarify relationships between methods and results. He also produced tutorial videos demonstrating software functions and analysis workflows, helping explain the team’s computational tools and their use. (12) Writing – He drafted and revised the Dry Lab Wiki sections and method documentation, explaining the scientific rationale, computational approaches, and key findings. He connected model predictions and RNA-seq analyses with the wet-lab experiments they were intended to inform. He integrated software descriptions, visualizations, and workflow records into the written account of the project. Throughout the documentation, he distinguished computational interpretation from experimental evidence and described limitations relevant to the reported conclusions. (13) Safety – He developed a reusable computational safety-assessment framework combining RNA-seq analysis, editor comparisons, site prioritization, and whole-body delivery modeling. The framework considered potential off-target editing, tissue exposure, and uncertainty in the underlying models. He used these analyses to organize safety-related questions and guide priorities for experimental follow-up. He documented the assessment’s limitations, distinguishing computational predictions from experimentally validated biological safety. His contribution supported a structured discussion of potential risks and the evidence still needed to evaluate them.

Specific Tasks
(1) Conceptualization He co-developed the initial project concept and research direction with the founding team members, establishing APOE4-targeted RNA editing as the central strategy for addressing Alzheimer's disease. He independently proposed and designed the major engineering frameworks of the ORCA system, including the multi-layer regulatory architecture, AAV9-based delivery strategy, and the Reporter/SONAR quantitative measurement system. He conceived and developed engineering strategies for astrocyte-specific expression, miRNA-responsive regulation, ASO-mediated reversible suppression, and Cre-loxP-mediated permanent shutdown. He also designed the overall integration of these modules into a coherent and controllable RNA-editing platform. He took primary responsibility for translating these engineering concepts into experimental strategies and coordinating their implementation throughout the project. (2) Background Research He conducted extensive literature research on Alzheimer's disease, APOE biology, programmable RNA editing, astrocyte-specific gene expression, regulatory RNA elements, and viral delivery systems. He investigated and compared engineering approaches involving the gfaABC1D promoter, miRNA-responsive elements, antisense oligonucleotides, Cre-loxP recombination, and AAV-based gene delivery. He reviewed relevant studies to evaluate the feasibility, advantages, and limitations of alternative strategies, providing the scientific basis for construct design, experimental planning, troubleshooting, and iterative engineering decisions. He also researched experimental techniques and analytical approaches relevant to reporter-based RNA-editing measurements and cellular functional validation. (3) Investigation He independently designed and conducted the majority of experiments involving the gfaABC1D promoter, miRNA-responsive elements, ASO-mediated regulation, the Reporter/SONAR system, AAV9 delivery, and cellular functional validation. He collaborated with Qu Xiaoyu on the initial construction of the GFA, MRE, and reporter plasmids, and independently completed the subsequent experimental investigations and validation of these modules. He performed mammalian cell culture, plasmid preparation, molecular cloning, transfection, flow cytometry, and other molecular and cellular biology procedures required for the engineering experiments. He independently carried out two rounds of AAV9 packaging and characterization, involving AAV-mCherry and AAV-ORCA constructs. His work included vector preparation, viral packaging, purification, qPCR-based titer determination, and preliminary delivery evaluation. He took full responsibility for experimental investigations using CCF-STTG1 astrocytes, including RNA-editing experiments, editing-efficiency measurements, coverslip preparation, fluorescence imaging, BODIPY-based lipid staining, and Aβ uptake-related ELISA assays. He received technical assistance during electroporation. He also participated in experimental troubleshooting and iterative optimization across these engineering modules. (4) Analysis He independently processed, analyzed, and interpreted experimental results from promoter characterization, MRE evaluation, ASO-mediated regulation, reporter-based RNA-editing measurements, and AAV characterization. He developed the quantitative analysis framework of the SONAR system, using fluorescent reporter signals, flow-cytometry measurements, and experimental controls to evaluate RNA-editing activity. He analyzed experimental outcomes to assess the performance of engineering modules, identify potential limitations, investigate unexpected results, and guide subsequent design iterations. He contributed to the interpretation of cellular functional validation results, including measurements related to intracellular lipid accumulation and Aβ uptake. He also integrated findings from different experimental modules to support system-level engineering decisions and the overall evaluation of ORCA. (5) Data Curation He took primary responsibility for collecting, organizing, processing, and maintaining experimental data generated by the wet-lab team. He systematically organized datasets from flow cytometry, molecular biology assays, AAV characterization, RNA-editing measurements, and cellular functional experiments. He consolidated raw experimental records, processed results, supporting documentation, and figure-related data for scientific analysis and Wiki presentation. He also coordinated data integration across experimental modules, ensuring consistency between original experimental records, quantitative analyses, and reported findings. (6) Notebook / Record Keeping He took primary responsibility for organizing and compiling the team's Wet Lab Notebook. He independently authored and standardized experimental protocols covering the major wet-lab procedures used throughout the project. He systematically documented experimental designs, procedures, conditions, observations, results, and engineering iterations. He organized experimental records and supporting materials to facilitate data traceability, reproducibility, and the preparation of scientific documentation. (7) Project Administration He served as Co-Leader and Head of Wet Lab, taking primary responsibility for the organization, management, and coordination of the team's experimental research. He coordinated wet-lab task allocation, experimental scheduling, research progress, and the implementation of experimental plans. He organized team discussions, supervised experimental progress, and facilitated communication and collaboration among members working on different project modules. He provided experimental training and technical guidance to team members and assisted in troubleshooting experimental difficulties. He maintained communication with principal investigators, laboratory personnel, and external collaborators regarding experimental planning, technical requirements, laboratory resources, and research progress. He also coordinated the procurement of experimental materials, communicated with suppliers, and helped resolve logistical and technical issues encountered during the project. He contributed to the overall coordination of project activities and the integration of experimental results into the team's broader research objectives. (8) Public Engagement He co-organized and participated in multiple scientific communication, educational outreach, and collaborative activities throughout the project. He participated in the Guangzhou iGEM exchange meeting, Grand APiC, and educational exchanges with high school students in Shenzhen, including activities involving SUSTech Affiliated High School. He worked with other team members to organize and conduct interviews with teachers, medical professionals, and university professors, gathering interdisciplinary perspectives relevant to the project. He also contributed to Synbio Challenges and collaborative educational initiatives, including the development of educational web content in collaboration with NAU-China. Through these activities, he contributed to promoting public understanding of synthetic biology, communicating the team's research, and facilitating scientific exchange and collaboration. He conceived and designed its quantitative evaluation framework, including the use of fluorescence-based readouts, reference controls, and calibration strategies to assess RNA-editing activity. He developed the associated analytical methodology and integrated the SONAR framework into the team's experimental measurement and data analysis workflow. He also evaluated and refined the framework based on experimental results to improve the interpretation and comparison of RNA-editing measurements. (10) Wiki Coding He took primary responsibility for the development and implementation of major sections of the team's Wiki. He implemented and organized the presentation of scientific content, experimental data, figures, and supporting materials across these pages. He also refined the structure, layout, and visual consistency of the corresponding Wiki sections to improve the accessibility, clarity, and coherence of the project's scientific documentation. (11) Visualization He independently established a unified AI-assisted scientific visualization workflow to maintain a consistent visual style across the project's figures and documentation. He designed the scientific concepts, visual structures, layouts, and detailed specifications for figures illustrating the ORCA architecture, regulatory mechanisms, AAV delivery strategy, experimental workflows, and quantitative results.
