PUF12 combines broader site coverage with stronger shared-site editing

Under the common screening procedure, PUF12 retained 12,908 candidate sites compared with 2,774 for PUF10. Their respective retained-set median rates were 13.99% and 12.75%. The longer recognition construct therefore accompanied a broader detected profile.

Target signal and candidate-site breadth jointly guide recognition and catalytic design.

Our ten-to-twelve-repeat comparison links the longer PUF construct to broader detected editing in the complete fusion editor. Together with the reported eight-to-ten-repeat specificity gain (Han et al., 2022), this result identifies repeat length as a context-dependent design variable. Scaffold geometry, RNA recruitment and catalytic preference jointly inform its selection.

The RNA-seq comparison established a broader PUF12 profile and higher rates at most shared sites. These measurements characterize cellular editing output; binding affinity is a separate property.

Shared-site rates clarify the count comparison

Among 2,221 jointly retained sites, PUF12 had a higher reported rate at 73.1%, with a median within-site increase of 3.02 percentage points. This comparison links rates at the same genomic allele keys within the jointly retained subset.

This shared-site comparison adds information beyond the medians of two different retained-site sets. PUF12's broader profile was accompanied by higher editing at most jointly detected sites, linking editing breadth with increased output within the shared subset. The paired-site analysis establishes the cellular output associated with each construct and provides a basis for investigating recruitment and catalytic contributions.

Retaining target signal while narrowing the profile

APOBEC-containing DNA base-editor studies reported reduced RNA off-target editing alongside retained DNA on-target activity (Grünewald et al., 2019; Zhou et al., 2019). They place control of unwanted RNA editing within an established engineering objective.

GVE retained 383 candidate sites, approximately 97.0% fewer than PUF12, alongside an apparent C388 signal of 64.98%. This combination identified GVE as a lead with a narrower detected editing profile and retained apparent target signal in HEK293T. Together with SNE’s higher apparent C388 profile, it defines complementary starting points for recognition-domain selection.

Within the BE3 study, wild-type constructs produced 90,509–192,088 RNA C-to-U sites, while BE3-R33A/K34A produced 7–26 (Grünewald et al., 2019). This within-study reduction provides a precedent for engineering RNA specificity. ORCA addresses that objective through joint measurement of target signal and candidate profiles.

Conserved local motifs support APOBEC-driven sequence preference

The recurrent −1 guanine preference across PUF variants supports a shared catalytic contribution to the local motif. We interpret this conservation as evidence that APOBEC is a major source of short-range sequence preference, while PUF variation mainly reshapes site recruitment. Published catalytic-scaffold comparisons provide supporting context for this interpretation (Han et al., 2025). The GVE shift further shows that recruitment can modify the local composition of detected sites.

This division connects catalytic motif preference with PUF-mediated recruitment.
