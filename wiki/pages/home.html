ORCA

On-target RNA Correction for Alzheimer’s Disease

Scroll to enter

Every turn looks familiar. None lead home.

Inside the brain.

Our story follows an astrocyte, then a single strand of RNA inside it.

Meet the astrocyte.

These cells support neurons and help handle proteins and lipids in the brain.

The instruction is APOE.

It encodes apolipoprotein E, a protein involved in moving lipids through the brain.

One letter changes the protein.

APOE4 RNA carries C at this site; APOE3 carries U. The difference changes one amino acid in the protein.

Amyloid can build up.

In human cell models, APOE4 astrocytes take up less amyloid-β than APOE3 astrocytes.

A higher risk of Alzheimer’s.

APOE4 increases disease risk. Carrying it does not mean someone will develop dementia.

Dementia affects millions.

57,000,000+
people were living with dementia worldwide in 2021
60–70%
of dementia cases are estimated to be attributable to Alzheimer disease
≈10 million
new dementia cases each year

What if we edit the RNA?

ORCA investigates targeted C-to-U editing to shift APOE4 RNA toward an APOE3-like state.

Could clearance recover?

ORCA asks whether targeted RNA editing can improve cellular function. Editing specificity, delivery and any benefit to cells must be tested.
