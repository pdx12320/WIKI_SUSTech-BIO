# HP scoped literature review — search and extraction record

Review date: **21 October 2026, Asia/Shanghai**. This is a retrospective, question-led review of selected literature alongside the current local team records, not a systematic review. It does not establish what the team knew when an earlier decision was made. Six sources were retained: four original research studies, one official report containing original survey research and one methodological guide.

Search engines were used for discovery, followed by original PMC full texts, publisher full texts and official PDFs. Searches also encountered abstract-only qualitative studies in Shandong and rural southwest China, a broader older-adult long-term-care preference study, a public digital-therapy preference study and an online affiliate-stigma study. These were not needed for the retained argument or did not offer enough accessible detail in the material inspected; no inaccessible result was filled in. This is a bounded selection, without an exhaustive search, risk-of-bias scoring, meta-analysis or claim of evidence saturation.

The extraction ledger is `sources.json`: stable IDs [5]–[10], primary URLs/DOIs, publication and available fieldwork dates, populations, methods, specific interpretation, checked sections and limits. Sources [1]–[4] already on HP were retained; they are not counted as newly reviewed sources.

## Original-report checks

- **[5]** Original Methods and Tables 1, 2, 6, 7 were inspected. “60” denotes the study's interview/group units, not 60 independently interviewed patients. The moderate-stage group includes patient–partner dyads; the severe-stage group uses partner reports. We do not turn qualitative mentions into a population ranking or transfer US priorities to local people.
- **[6]** Recruitment and data collection identify four Chaoyang service centers, 10 caregiver interviews and 29 workers in groups. Caregiver fieldwork was September–December 2017; worker groups January–February 2018. The local purposive study supplies context, not prevalence or an evaluated ORCA service model.
- **[7]** Design, Table 1 and care-burden tables were inspected. There were 1,694 collected questionnaires and 1,675 retained records. Respondents were carers reporting on probable-AD cases, not 1,675 direct patient interviews. Health-center, urban and education-related selection limits were retained. No published percentage is pooled with the ORCA questionnaire.
- **[8]** Methods, exclusions, Tables 2–3 and Figures 2–3 were inspected. 180/179 caregiver/neurologist completers became 137/161 retained respondents. Hypothetical attributes concerned amyloid-targeting therapies; cost and quality-of-life gains were held equal. We use differing stakeholder priorities, without importing its utilities, acceptable-risk estimates or treatment schedule into ORCA. Funding is stated on the public page.
- **[9]** The official original 16.3 MB PDF was downloaded into a task-specific temporary directory after the browser reader rejected its size. Chapter 2 pp. 27–28, methodology pp. 55–56 and Table 1 p. 64 were inspected; pp. 28 and 55 were rendered for visual checking. Importantly, the “China 15” on p. 64 is a **missing-weight count**, not a national respondent count. No such country count or headline global percentage was introduced into HP. 34,619 is the base answering at least one stigma item, not the denominator of every question.
- **[10]** The June 2020 final PDF, Sections II.B–F, Figure 1 and sampling tables were inspected. It is methods guidance, not an empirical patient-needs source or an assertion that ORCA has met a regulatory standard.

Fieldwork dates for [5], [8] and exact survey dates for [9] were not established in the inspected methods. Publication dates are recorded separately and not substituted for collection dates.
