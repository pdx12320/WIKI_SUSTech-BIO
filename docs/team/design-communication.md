## Our Design Principles

We use three principles to guide our work and carry them into our visual identity, team materials, and public communication. They guide [how our identity develops](#building-an-identity), [how we apply it across media](#design-in-action), and [how we invite public participation](#science-meets-people).

Rams’ principle gives us a useful reference point: design should clarify what people are encountering. We adapt that idea to scientific communication through clarity, a consistent identity, and public participation.

Source: [Vitsœ, “Good design”](https://www.vitsoe.com/eu/about/good-design).

### Scientific Clarity

We design for understanding. Graphics, color, typography, and information hierarchy help readers approach complex biomedical ideas while preserving the scientific detail they need.

Our Wiki visual explorations, project marks, and science-led illustrations are made to clarify an idea before they decorate it. The [early Wiki style explorations](#wiki-home-style-explorations) show how we explored these ideas in page compositions.

### A Consistent Identity

We build a recognizable identity across the places where the team works and meets people. From our early marks and REWIRE to ORCA, the logo, whale mascot, color language, and graphic elements have developed into a shared visual system.

The same language appears in our Wiki, team clothing, flags, meeting backgrounds, event materials, and small objects. Consistency lets each piece stand on its own while still belonging to the same team.

### Science for Everyone

We use design to make science approachable and participatory. Everyday visual cues, inviting interactions, and clear social-media storytelling create more ways for people to understand and respond to questions related to Alzheimer's disease.

The goal is not simply to publish information. It is to make room for recognition, response, and conversation.

| Color | The five-color palette above is the reference for our visual identity. Deep blue provides structure, violet and lavender provide supporting tones, and blush and coral pink add warmth. |
| Typography | Headings, body text, and display text use clear levels so readers can move through both scientific and public-facing content. |

| Cross-media consistency | Logos, colors, graphic elements, and information hierarchy are carried across the Wiki, clothing, flags, and event communication. |

Early logo explorations record where the visual direction began; REWIRE captures a turning point in the language; ORCA brings the whale, curve, and team identity into one system; and the Wiki applies that system to scientific content.

### ORCA

By the third version, the project name had become ORCA. The killer whale became the central image, the mascot’s headwear was refined, and the logo was adjusted to make the ORCA identity clearer.

The mascot extends the identity into a more personal form. It gives the team a character that can appear in explanatory graphics, event materials, and everyday communication without losing its connection to the research.

## Design in Action

We bring [the evolving ORCA identity](#building-an-identity) into the spaces where the team works, presents, and meets the public. A shared visual language becomes something people can wear, use, and recognize across these settings.

Team clothing turns the identity into something people wear together. The early hooded-jacket concept, second-version short-sleeve T-shirt, and third-version varsity jacket show the design developing over time, while two flag versions test the same language at a larger scale. Meeting backgrounds keep the identity present online, and event materials adapt it to different audiences and settings.

Stickers and other small objects—including blind-box cards, refrigerator magnets, and keychains—make the visual language easier to carry into daily conversations. Across these formats, the work keeps a clear relationship between scientific content, team identity, and the people encountering it.

A WORLDPROOF Hackathon participant badge shows the identity in an external event setting, while the mascot personality stickers turn the same visual language into small, expressive objects.

## Science Meets People

The principle of [Science for Everyone](#science-for-everyone) carries through into our public-facing work, connecting biomedical research with experiences that people can recognize in their own lives.

### Time Capsule

“Time Capsule” turns a difficult topic into an invitation to remember, share, and respond. It shows how a visual story can move beyond one-way explanation and create a space for personal connection.

The mailbox, writing materials, and visual prompts make this invitation tangible. Our “Time Capsule” (时光信箱) activity invites people to write down something they do not want to forget and keep a letter for a future opening date. The mascot stickers carry the same visual identity into the activity table.

The handwritten cards bring participants' own words into this visual story, turning a designed invitation into a personal response.

### Aphasia Map

<div class="design-story-layout">
  <div class="design-story-layout__row">
    <div class="design-story-layout__copy">
      <p>“Aphasia Map” brings the experience of language loss into view. By connecting an abstract health issue with concrete human experience, it gives public audiences a more accessible way to understand and discuss Alzheimer's disease.</p>
      <p>The illustrated instruction board turns this idea into a communication game. Participants select an object card, avoid its name and the listed words, and use gestures or alternative descriptions to help a partner identify it. Three clear steps guide people through the activity.</p>
