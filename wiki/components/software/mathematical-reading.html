From Rates to Trajectories

The balance above can be read at three mathematical levels: coupled states, accumulated change and a total across compartments. These general identities explain the notation behind a dynamical model.

A shared state vector

Collect the time-dependent states into a vector, with one component for each quantity being tracked. The vector function f collects their rates of change.

x(t)=(x1(t),…,xn(t))T dxdt=f(t,x;θ),x(t0)=x0

Here, n is the number of states, t denotes time, and θ denotes the parameter vector. The initial condition x0 specifies the states at the starting time t0.

A trajectory accumulates change

Integrating the rate equation gives the corresponding trajectory from its initial condition. This relation connects the instantaneous balance to the time courses shown in the figures.

x(t)=x0+∫t0tf(τ,x(τ);θ)dτ

The integration variable τ runs from the starting time to the time being evaluated. Each rate component has units of its corresponding state per unit time.

Internal transfers cancel in a total balance

For compartments tracking the same conserved quantity in compatible units, internal transfers appear once as an inflow and once as an outflow. Summing these balances therefore cancels those internal terms.

Q(t)=∑i=1mqi(t) dQdt=Jexternal,in−Jexternal,out+∑i=1m(Pi−Li)

Here, qi is an amount in compartment i, and Q is its total across m compartments. External fluxes, production Pi and loss Li account for changes in that total.

This sum applies to compatible amounts, rather than every entry of a mixed state vector. Concentrations require compartment-volume weighting before they can be combined into a total amount.
