ORCA

On-target RNA Correction of APOE4 for Alzheimer’s Disease

Scroll to enter

Brain

Alzheimer’s disease progressively damages brain cells and their connections, leading to memory loss and declining thinking abilities.

Astrocyte

Astrocytes support neurons and help regulate brain metabolism. Changes in their lipid handling and inflammatory responses are linked to Alzheimer’s disease.

APOE4 RNA

Astrocytes produce APOE to transport cholesterol and other lipids. APOE4 can disturb this balance, weakening metabolic support for neurons.

The target is the first C in CGC. Changing it to U produces UGC, corresponding to Arg112→Cys112 in mature APOE.

Astrocyte · Aβ

In human cell models, APOE4 astrocytes take up less amyloid-β than APOE3 astrocytes.

APOE4 and APOE3 differ at just one amino acid, Arg112 versus Cys112. Their effects on lipid handling and Alzheimer’s disease risk differ markedly.

What if we can
reverse it?

We aim to change APOE4 RNA to an APOE3 sequence without altering genomic DNA.

PUF recognizes and binds the target RNA. APOBEC converts the target C to U.

We design ORCA as a modular RNA editing platform, coupling programmable recognition with catalytic engineering.

We also investigate GFA, MRE and ASO designs for expression control, explore AAV9 delivery, and develop SONAR for editing-responsive fluorescence measurements.

We integrate wet-lab evidence with foundation models and structural modeling to guide refinement of the PUF–APOBEC RNA editing system.

Our goal is greater editing specificity while preserving useful target activity.
