From Reads to Comparable Measurements
Quality and multiple testing

Base and mapping quality thresholds were raised above Q30, implemented as BQ≥31 and MAPQ≥31. Phred quality relates to predicted error probability through the following relationship (Illumina, 2014):

Q=−10log⁡10Perror,Perror=10−Q/10. Q=-10\log_{10}P_{\mathrm{error}},\qquad P_{\mathrm{error}}=10^{-Q/10}.

At each site, one-sided pooled-read Fisher tests compared treatment with each of the three controls. The largest p-value entered Benjamini–Hochberg correction, with q≤0.05 required (Benjamini & Hochberg, 1995). For m ordered p-values:

q(i)=min⁡{1,minj≥imp(j)j}. q_{(i)}=\min\left\{1,\min_{j\geq i}\frac{m\,p_{(j)}}{j}\right\}.
Allele-specific rates

We added an allele-specific recount in every library. Reads had to satisfy the quality thresholds and unique-alignment requirement; duplicate and other excluded alignment classes contributed no support. Reference and target-alternative observations were counted separately:

ri,k=ALTi,ktargetREFi,k+ALTi,ktarget,r̃i=median⁡k=1,…,4ri,k. r_{i,k}=\frac{\mathrm{ALT}^{\mathrm{target}}_{i,k}} {\mathrm{REF}_{i,k}+\mathrm{ALT}^{\mathrm{target}}_{i,k}}, \qquad \widetilde r_i=\operatorname{median}_{k=1,\ldots,4}r_{i,k}.

Each treated replicate required target ALT≥20, depth≥100 and rate≥0.005. The four-replicate median defined the exported site rate, giving replicates equal standing when library depths differed.

Variant exclusion and replicate consistency

Four HEK293T SNP catalogues were merged after conversion to GRCh38 and reference-allele checks. Exclusion used exact chromosome–position–REF–ALT matches:

Smerged=⋃j=14Sj,Celigible=Ccandidate\Smerged. S_{\mathrm{merged}}=\bigcup_{j=1}^{4}S_j, \qquad C_{\mathrm{eligible}}=C_{\mathrm{candidate}}\setminus S_{\mathrm{merged}}.

Each control group required a median allele rate and median absolute deviation of at most 0.02, with no formal caller detections. Median differences between APOBEC-only or PUF-only controls and the mock control also had to remain at or below 0.02.

Treatment candidates required detection in all four replicates and a median absolute deviation of at most 0.05. These consistency checks complemented the pooled-read significance test.

Matched-site comparison

Matching chromosome, position, REF and ALT identified 2,221 shared sites. At each site, we compared the four-replicate median rates:

Δri=r̃PUF12,i−r̃PUF10,i,fhigher=#{i:Δri>0}2,221. \Delta r_i=\widetilde r_{\mathrm{PUF12},i}-\widetilde r_{\mathrm{PUF10},i}, \qquad f_{\mathrm{higher}}=\frac{\#\{i:\Delta r_i>0\}}{2,221}.
Callable-cytosine normalization

A fixed reference contained 899,942 callable cytosines passing the quality, strand, SNP and sequence filters. Reference sites required depth≥100 in all four PUF12 libraries and depth≥20 in all twelve control libraries.

Each editor's candidates were intersected with this reference. Chromosome densities were then scaled to a relative distribution:

dg,c=Ng,cCc,Pg,c=100%×dg,c∑c′dg,c′. d_{g,c}=\frac{N_{g,c}}{C_c}, \qquad P_{g,c}=100\%\times\frac{d_{g,c}}{\sum_{c'}d_{g,c'}}.

Here, N is the candidate count within the reference and C is the callable-cytosine count. Undefined denominators remained missing values.

From Features to a Specificity Score
Structural features and endpoint predictions

We extracted CP features from AlphaFold 3 outputs (Abramson et al., 2024) and tested alternative mappings: CP clustering, high-variance subsets, intraprotein contacts and protein–RNA interface contacts. These representations supplied classification and continuous regression models for each endpoint. With contact probability matrix PP and feature mapping ϕ\phi, the model relationships were:

xCP=ϕ(P),ŷj=hj(xCP),Ĉj=fj(xCP),j∈{295,388,871}. x_{\mathrm{CP}}=\phi(P),\qquad \hat y_j=h_j(x_{\mathrm{CP}}),\qquad \hat C_j=f_j(x_{\mathrm{CP}}),\qquad j\in\{295,388,871\}.

Here, hjh_j denotes classification and fjf_j denotes continuous regression. We converted the regression outputs into a common specificity objective:

Ŝ=Ĉ388−Ĉ295+Ĉ8712. \hat S=\hat C_{388}-\frac{\hat C_{295}+\hat C_{871}}{2}.

This formulation retained separate target and bystander predictions while providing one ranking for candidate selection.

Frozen sequence representations

We froze ESM-2 (Lin et al., 2023) and extracted global and local representations for the candidate sequences. Each representation supplied ridge regression models for C295, C388 and C871, followed by the same specificity calculation. The prediction step can be written as:

x=ϕESM(a),Ĉj=bj+βj𝖳x, x=\phi_{\mathrm{ESM}}(a),\qquad \hat C_j=b_j+\beta_j^{\mathsf T}x,

where aa is the amino-acid sequence and ϕESM\phi_{\mathrm{ESM}} denotes the selected frozen representation. The comparison used the same 66 complete-label constructs, grouped evaluation and parameter-selection rule for the CP, global ESM and local ESM models. This kept the prediction task fixed while changing the feature source.

Evolutionary prior and regularized fitting

Adapting the evolutionary-plus-assay strategy of Hsu et al. (2022), we combined reference-centred amino-acid one-hot features with a frozen ESM-2 masked mutation score. Its 493×20493\times20 encoding assigned −1-1 to the reference residue and +1+1 to the replacement at each mutated position. For mutation set MM, the score was:

s(a)=∑i∈M[logp(ai∣aref,i←[MASK])−logp(aref,i∣aref,i←[MASK])]. s(a)=\sum_{i\in M}\left[ \log p(a_i\mid a_{\mathrm{ref},i\leftarrow[\mathrm{MASK}]}) -\log p(a_{\mathrm{ref},i}\mid a_{\mathrm{ref},i\leftarrow[\mathrm{MASK}]}) \right].

Each endpoint used ridge regression (Hoerl & Kennard, 1970), its labelled training set OjO_j and training-derived score standardization:

(β̂j,b̂j)=arg⁡minβ,b{∑n∈Oj(Cnj−b−xn𝖳β)2+λ‖β‖22},x=[xonehot,ws̃]. (\hat\beta_j,\hat b_j)=\underset{\beta,b}{\arg\min} \left\{\sum_{n\in O_j}(C_{nj}-b-x_n^{\mathsf T}\beta)^2+\lambda\lVert\beta\rVert_2^2\right\}, \qquad x=[x_{\mathrm{onehot}},\,w\widetilde{s}].

Missing endpoints were masked during fitting, and identical sequences remained in the same group. Inner grouped validation selected λ\lambda and score weight ww; outer holdouts assessed predictions.

The Layer-Specific Score
Comparing representation depth

We read layers 1–12, masked each mutated position in the reference sequence, and applied the same frozen vocabulary head:

sℓ(a)=∑i∈M[logpℓ(ai∣aref,i←[MASK])−logpℓ(aref,i∣aref,i←[MASK])],ℓ∈{1,…,12}. s_{\ell}(a)=\sum_{i\in M}\left[ \log p_{\ell}(a_i\mid a_{\mathrm{ref},i\leftarrow[\mathrm{MASK}]}) -\log p_{\ell}(a_{\mathrm{ref},i}\mid a_{\mathrm{ref},i\leftarrow[\mathrm{MASK}]}) \right],\qquad \ell\in\{1,\ldots,12\}.

Layers 1–11 passed through the frozen final encoder normalization and vocabulary head; layer 12 used its native normalized output. Each layer's score was paired with identical one-hot features, samples, groups and ridge parameter searches. A nested evaluation selected the layer and regression parameters together within inner validation, testing the complete selection procedure. This separation of model selection from outer evaluation addresses selection bias in cross-validation (Varma & Simon, 2006).

From Structural Context to Candidate Sequences
Conditional sequence generation

ProteinMPNN (Dauparas et al., 2022) and LigandMPNN (Dauparas et al., 2025) generated sequences from the same PUF backbone coordinates BB. LigandMPNN additionally incorporated the fixed RNA atomic environment RR:

a∼pProteinMPNN(a∣B),a∼pLigandMPNN(a∣B,R). a\sim p_{\mathrm{ProteinMPNN}}(a\mid B),\qquad a\sim p_{\mathrm{LigandMPNN}}(a\mid B,R).

Each model generated 10,000 sequences. The global screen selected the most frequent non-reference residue when its frequency exceeded the reference frequency and reached 0.80.8. Scaffold screening excluded 36 TRM positions and placed each selected replacement individually into the reference sequence. Exact consensus required both models to nominate the same substitution.

A parallel TRM-only comparison fixed all 457 scaffold residues and generated 10,000 sequences per model. Each nominated TRM substitution was also scored on the reference background, averaging eight shared decoding orders.

The existing scaffold selection rule

The screening strategy drew on inverse folding with structural and evolutionary constraints (Fei et al., 2025). We completed PyDSSP three-state annotation and recognized its loop code as -. This restored the existing loop threshold of 0.50.5 alongside the global threshold of 0.80.8. For the most frequent non-reference replacement aa at position ii, the complete rule was:

retain⁡(i→a)⇔fi(a)>fi(aref,i)∧[fi(a)≥0.8∨(i∈ℒ∧fi(a)≥0.5)], \operatorname{retain}(i\to a) \iff f_i(a)>f_i(a_{\mathrm{ref},i}) \;\land\; \left[f_i(a)\ge0.8\;\lor\; \left(i\in\mathcal L\;\land\;f_i(a)\ge0.5\right)\right],

where fif_i is the sampled residue frequency and ℒ\mathcal L contains annotated loop positions. We reapplied the rule to the same 10,000 samples per model. The screen again excluded all 36 TRM positions and exported individual substitutions on the reference background, retaining exact substitution identity for consensus comparisons.

Quantifying Changes in Predicted Contacts
Paired-substrate contact probabilities

We also extracted contact probability (CP) from AlphaFold 3 outputs, taking the larger directional value for each protein-residue–RNA-nucleotide pair:

CP¯v,r,i=1Nv,r∑m=1Nv,rmax⁡(Pi,+1(m),P+1,i(m)),ΔCPv,i=CP¯v,GCA,i−CP¯v,GCG,i. \overline{CP}_{v,r,i} =\frac{1}{N_{v,r}}\sum_{m=1}^{N_{v,r}} \max\left(P^{(m)}_{i,+1},P^{(m)}_{+1,i}\right), \qquad \Delta CP_{v,i} =\overline{CP}_{v,\mathrm{GCA},i} -\overline{CP}_{v,\mathrm{GCG},i}.

Here, vv identifies the construct, rr the RNA context, ii the protein residue and Nv,rN_{v,r} the available predicted models. Geometric contact frequencies described observed arrangements across predictions; CP supplied the continuous paired-substrate features.

Parent-corrected contact differences

We extended paired GCG/GCA predictions to the variant panel and changed the comparison from substrate difference to a parent-corrected double difference:

ΔΔCPv,i=ΔCPv,i−ΔCPparent,i. \Delta\Delta CP_{v,i} =\Delta CP_{v,i}-\Delta CP_{\mathrm{parent},i}.

The correction was calculated residue by residue across the 199-residue scaffold. Positive ΔΔCP\Delta\Delta CP denotes a shift towards A-associated contacts relative to the parent. The resulting profiles preserved the location and direction of changes throughout the interface.

Deviation from the parental representation

Alongside the paired complexes, we ran new AlphaFold 3 predictions containing only the protein chain (Abramson et al., 2024). Contact features were averaged within each computational seed and then equally across seeds. Parent seeds alone defined the projection basis and normalization scale:

DM(v)=1K∑k=1K(zv,k−μparent,kσparent,k)2. D_M(v)=\sqrt{\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K} \left(\frac{z_{v,k}-\mu_{\mathrm{parent},k}} {\sigma_{\mathrm{parent},k}}\right)^2}.

Here, zz denotes projected contact features, KK the retained dimensions, and μ\mu and σ\sigma the parental projection mean and standard deviation. Each input condition used its own parent reference. The complex and protein-only distances were compared side by side.

From Contact Patterns to Structural Groups
Seed-balanced contact vectors and similarities

For 11 constructs, 56 computational seeds and 280 predicted structures, we extracted protein-internal CP matrices. Each seed's five predictions were symmetrized and averaged, followed by equal averaging across seeds:

xv,(i,j)=1Sv∑s=1Sv15∑m=15Pij(v,s,m)+Pji(v,s,m)2,|i−j|≥4. x_{v,(i,j)}= \frac{1}{S_v}\sum_{s=1}^{S_v} \frac{1}{5}\sum_{m=1}^{5} \frac{P^{(v,s,m)}_{ij}+P^{(v,s,m)}_{ji}}{2}, \qquad |i-j|\geq4.

Here, SvS_v is the number of seeds for construct vv. The resulting representation contained 19,110 nonlocal residue pairs, including 766 variable pairs. We retained the CP probability scale and compared clustering methods on these construct vectors.

Following the spectral clustering framework of Ng et al. (2002), we connected constructs through radial-basis similarities:

Auv=exp⁡[−‖xu−xv‖22σ2],σ=median⁡u<v‖xu−xv‖. A_{uv}=\exp\left[-\frac{\lVert x_u-x_v\rVert^2}{2\sigma^2}\right], \qquad \sigma=\operatorname{median}_{u<v}\lVert x_u-x_v\rVert.

Cluster count was selected from two, three or four groups using silhouette in the selected CP space (Rousseeuw, 1987). We also compared partition recurrence with cluster count fixed or reselected during seed resampling.

Feature variance and partition recurrence

We ranked contact pairs by variance across the 11 construct means and compared the highest-variance subsets with the full representation:

Vf=1n−1∑v=1n(xv,f−x¯f)2,n=11. V_f=\frac{1}{n-1}\sum_{v=1}^{n}(x_{v,f}-\overline{x}_f)^2, \qquad n=11.

The tested subsets contained 77, 154 or 230 pairs. Each workflow underwent 200 bootstrap replicates, resampling whole seed groups within each construct. Feature selection, representation fitting and cluster-count selection were repeated within each replicate (bootstrap methodology: Efron, 1979). Complete-partition recurrence was calculated as:

R=1200∑b=1200𝟏[ARI(Lb,L0)=1]. R=\frac{1}{200}\sum_{b=1}^{200} \mathbf{1}\!\left[\operatorname{ARI}(L_b,L_0)=1\right].

Here, L0L_0 is a workflow's baseline partition, LbL_b its resampled partition, and ARI the adjusted Rand index (Hubert & Arabie, 1985). An ARI of one indicates identical complete partitions. As a complementary control, confidence-filtered Ward clustering (Ward, 1963) retained pairs whose two endpoints had minimum construct-mean Cα pLDDT of at least 70 (confidence-score background: Jumper et al., 2021). The endpoint-confidence mask remained fixed across all 200 bootstrap replicates.

Connecting RNA Context to a Prediction Head
Encoder and task head

Following advice from PI Zefeng Wang, we adopted the RNA language model LAMAR as a pretrained encoder. A task-specific prediction head connected its contextual representations to editing labels (Transformer background: Vaswani et al., 2017):

h=Eθ(x),ŷ=gϕ(h). h = E_{\theta}(x), \qquad \hat{y} = g_{\phi}(h).

Here, xx is the RNA sequence, EθE_{\theta} is the LAMAR encoder, hh is its contextual representation, and gϕg_{\phi} is the task head. This architecture separated general sequence learning from supervised prediction of editing-associated labels.

{% endmacro %} {% macro rna_adaptation() %}
Learning from Masked Human Transcripts
Masked-language modelling objective

We continued masked language modelling on human transcripts derived from GRCh38 and GENCODE release 50. GENCODE provides the reference gene-annotation framework (Mudge et al., 2025). The encoder learned to predict masked nucleotides from their surrounding sequence (masked-language-modelling background: Devlin et al., 2019):

ℒMLM=−1|M|∑i∈Mlog⁡pθ(xi∣xmask). \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{MLM}} = -\frac{1}{|M|}\sum_{i\in M} \log p_{\theta}\!\left(x_i\mid x^{\mathrm{mask}}\right).

MM denotes the masked positions, and xmaskx^{\mathrm{mask}} denotes the masked input sequence. Updating the encoder with this objective adapted its parameters to the human transcript distribution.

{% endmacro %} {% macro rna_regions() %}
Defining a Positive Region
Regional labels and class probabilities

Stage I used a classification head to predict whether an input region contained a labelled positive:

yW=𝟏(∃i∈W:yi=1),pW=softmax⁡(gI(Eθ(W)))1. y_W = \mathbf{1}\!\left(\exists i\in W:\ y_i=1\right), \qquad p_W = \operatorname{softmax}\!\left(g_{\mathrm{I}}(E_{\theta}(W))\right)_1.

WW denotes the input region, yWy_W its label, and pWp_W the positive-class output. Stage II used 101-nt cytidine-centred contexts for local prediction. The two tasks were trained separately from the human-adapted encoder; Stage II initialized directly from the human-pretraining checkpoint.

The regional partitions contained 3,835 training regions, 213 validation regions and 212 test regions. This decomposition gave each model a distinct prediction target and input scale.

{% endmacro %} {% macro rna_finetuning() %}
Adapting the Encoder to Regional Labels
Frozen and fully fine-tuned objectives

The frozen configuration trained the regional prediction head while keeping the encoder parameters fixed. Full fine-tuning updated both the encoder and the head. Original LAMAR configurations and random initialization provided additional reference conditions.

ϕ̂frozen=arg⁡minϕℒcls(θ0,ϕ),(θ̂,ϕ̂)full=arg⁡minθ,ϕℒcls(θ,ϕ). \widehat\phi_{\mathrm{frozen}}= \underset{\phi}{\arg\min}\,\mathcal L_{\mathrm{cls}}(\theta_0,\phi), \qquad (\widehat\theta,\widehat\phi)_{\mathrm{full}}= \underset{\theta,\phi}{\arg\min}\,\mathcal L_{\mathrm{cls}}(\theta,\phi).

Here, θ0\theta_0 is the human-adapted encoder initialization, ϕ\phi denotes the head parameters, and ℒcls\mathcal L_{\mathrm{cls}} is the regional classification objective.

Validation AP guided regional hyperparameter and checkpoint selection, with Optuna used for the parameter search. Training loss tracked fitting to the training labels; validation AP assessed the ranking of labelled regions. We interpreted precision–recall performance in the context of positive-label prevalence (Davis & Goadrich, 2006; Saito & Rehmsmeier, 2015).

{% endmacro %} {% macro rna_ranking() %}
Concentrating Scores on Supported Sites
Multi-positive ranking objective

Stage II acquired a scalar centre-token head that assigned each cytidine a ranking score sis_i. We trained the encoder and scoring head with a multi-positive log-sum-exp ranking loss:

ℒrank=−log⁡[∑i∈Pexp⁡(si)∑j∈Cexp⁡(sj)]. \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{rank}} = -\log\left[ \frac{\sum_{i\in P}\exp(s_i)} {\sum_{j\in C}\exp(s_j)} \right].

CC contains the cytidine candidates included in training for a region, and PP contains its labelled positives. The loss increases the relative score weight assigned to the positive set. We changed the output interpretation, training objective and checkpoint criterion together, selecting the model by validation Hit@10.
